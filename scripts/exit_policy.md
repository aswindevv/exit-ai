§1.1 Purpose and scope
This policy defines how an employee's exit from the company is planned, approved, and
completed. It covers resignation, notice, knowledge transfer, clearance by HR, the
reporting manager, IT and Finance, return of company assets, final settlement, exit
documentation, and the obligations that continue after the last working day. It applies
to the whole exit process regardless of the reason for leaving.

§1.2 Document control
This is the company's controlled Employee Exit and Offboarding Policy. It is owned and
maintained by Human Resources, who are the only authority permitted to amend it. Where
an individual employment contract, a signed addendum, or local statutory law states
terms different from this policy, that contract or law takes precedence for that
employee. All other cases follow this document.

§1.3 Who this policy applies to
This policy applies to all confirmed employees, employees serving probation, and
fixed-term employees, across all departments and locations. Contractors and vendor
staff are offboarded under their agency agreement, though the asset return and
confidentiality rules in this policy apply to them in the same way.

§1.4 Definitions used in this policy
"Notice period" is the time between HR accepting a resignation and the last working
day. "Last working day" (LWD) is the final day of employment. "Knowledge transfer" (KT)
is the documented handover of work to colleagues. "Clearance" is a department's
confirmation that it has nothing outstanding against the employee. "Final settlement"
is the closing payment made after all clearances.

§1.5 Roles in the exit process
The employee submits the resignation, completes KT, returns assets, and provides exit
documents. The reporting manager approves the resignation and signs off KT. HR runs the
overall process, schedules the exit interview, and issues letters. IT revokes access and
collects IT assets. Finance settles dues and recovers outstanding amounts.

§2.1 Resignation notice period
Employees must submit written resignation with a minimum notice period of 30 days,
except during the probation period where 15 days' notice is sufficient. The last
working day is set by HR after manager approval and cannot be shortened without a
signed early-release waiver from the manager.

§2.2 Submitting a resignation
A resignation must be submitted in writing through the employee exit portal, which
records the submission date and starts the notice period once HR accepts it. A verbal
resignation is not valid and does not start the notice period. The resignation should
state the intended last working day and the reason for leaving.

§2.3 Manager review and acceptance
The reporting manager reviews the resignation and either approves it or returns it with
comments. HR then formally accepts it and confirms the last working day. If the manager
is unavailable or does not respond, HR may route the approval to the next appropriate
approver so the exit is not delayed.

§2.4 Withdrawing a resignation
A resignation may be withdrawn only before HR accepts it, and only with the written
agreement of the reporting manager and HR. Once the last working day is confirmed and
clearance has begun, withdrawal is treated as a new request and is not guaranteed.

§2.5 Notice period shortfall
An employee who leaves before serving the full notice period has a shortfall. Unless
the shortfall is waived in writing, pay in lieu of the unserved days is recovered from
the final settlement. A shortfall that is not waived may also affect the wording of the
relieving letter.

§2.6 Early release and garden leave
The manager may grant early release using a signed early-release waiver, which moves
the last working day earlier without creating a shortfall. The company may alternatively
place an employee on garden leave, where the employee remains employed and paid through
the notice period but is not required to attend work.

§2.7 Involuntary exit and redundancy
Where employment ends at the company's initiative, notice and severance follow the
employment contract and local law rather than the notice rules in §2.1. All other parts
of this policy, including clearance, asset return, settlement, and post-exit
obligations, apply in the same way as a resignation.

§2.8 Abandonment of employment
An employee who stops attending work without an approved resignation or leave is
treated as having abandoned employment. HR will attempt written contact before closing
the record. Clearance still applies, unreturned assets are recovered at replacement
cost, and the relieving letter may be withheld.

§3.1 Leave during the notice period
Leave during the notice period requires manager approval and is granted only where it
does not put knowledge transfer or clearance at risk. Approved leave does not reduce
the notice period; the last working day moves later by the number of days taken unless
HR confirms otherwise in writing.

§3.2 Leave encashment
Unused earned leave is encashed in the final settlement at the rate defined by the
employee's salary structure. Casual, sick, and other non-encashable leave categories
lapse on the last working day. Leave taken beyond the accrued balance is recovered as a
deduction.

§4.1 Knowledge transfer (KT)
Every exiting employee must complete a knowledge-transfer plan with their manager,
covering ongoing projects, credentials to be handed over, and open action items. KT
sessions should be scheduled within the first week of the notice period and completed
at least 3 working days before the last working day.

§4.2 KT sessions and scheduling
The manager creates KT tasks and the system books a calendar session for each one with
the employee, the manager, and the receiving colleague. Employees are sent reminders as
each KT task approaches its due date. Sessions missed without being rescheduled are
reported to HR as a clearance risk.

§4.3 Manager and KT sign-off
The reporting manager must review and approve the knowledge-transfer document before
the IT clearance stage can begin. An unapproved KT plan will delay the employee's
final clearance and settlement.

§4.4 KT gaps and rework
If the manager's review finds the handover incomplete, the specific gaps are recorded
and returned to the employee with a revised due date. The KT stage stays open until the
gaps are closed. Repeated unresolved gaps are escalated to HR, who may hold the
relieving letter until the handover is adequate.

§4.5 Handover of credentials and accounts
Shared credentials, service accounts, signing keys, and administrative rights held by
the employee must be transferred to a named colleague and then rotated by IT. Employees
must never share personal passwords. Any account that cannot be transferred must be
listed in the KT document so IT can reassign or close it.

§5.1 Clearance sequence
An employee's exit clearance must be completed by HR, Finance, and IT before the final
relieving letter is issued. Clearances are processed in the order: HR resignation
acceptance, manager and KT sign-off, IT deprovisioning, then Finance settlement.

§5.2 HR clearance
HR clearance confirms that the resignation is accepted, the last working day is agreed,
the exit interview is scheduled or completed, and all required exit documents have been
received and validated. HR clearance is the first stage and unlocks the stages that
follow it.

§5.3 Manager clearance
Manager clearance confirms that knowledge transfer is complete and signed off, that
ongoing work has been reassigned, and that any team-held assets or access have been
returned. The manager cannot clear an employee while KT gaps remain open.

§5.4 IT clearance
IT clearance confirms that all IT assets have been returned and checked, that email,
SSO, VPN, and internal tool access have been revoked, and that shared credentials have
been rotated. IT clearance cannot begin until the manager has signed off KT.

§5.5 Finance clearance
Finance clearance confirms that salary advances, travel advances, loans, corporate card
balances, and outstanding reimbursement claims have been settled or scheduled for
recovery. Finance is the final clearance stage and closes immediately before the
relieving letter is issued.

§5.6 Blocked or pending clearance
A clearance stage is blocked when a required item is still outstanding, for example an
asset not returned, an unsigned confidentiality acknowledgement, or access that has not
yet been revoked. The blocking items are listed for the employee, and the stage reopens
automatically once each item is resolved.

§5.7 Clearance service levels and escalation
Each clearance stage has a target completion time inside the notice period. A stage that
passes its due date without action is escalated to HR, who may reassign it to another
approver. Escalation is a process control and does not by itself reflect on the
departing employee.

§5.8 Checking your clearance status
Employees can see their current stage, outstanding tasks, and due dates in the employee
exit portal at any time during the notice period. The portal is the authoritative view
of what is still pending. Questions about a task should go first to the owning
department, and then to HR.

§6.1 Company assets
All company assets, including laptops, monitors, mobile devices, and access cards,
must be returned to IT on or before the last working day. Unreturned assets are
deducted from the final settlement at replacement cost.

§6.2 Asset condition and damage
Returned assets are inspected by IT and recorded against the employee's asset list.
Normal wear and tear is accepted. Damage beyond normal use, and missing accessories such
as chargers, docks, or carry cases, are charged at repair or replacement cost through
the final settlement.

§6.3 Remote employees and asset shipping
Employees who work remotely must request a return shipping kit from IT at least 7 days
before the last working day and ship the assets so they arrive no later than that day.
The company pays reasonable return shipping costs. Assets are treated as returned only
once IT records them as received.

§6.4 Personal devices and company data
Employees who accessed company systems from a personal device must remove company data,
sign out of company accounts, and uninstall company applications, including any mobile
device management profile, on or before the last working day. IT may require written
confirmation that this has been done.

§7.1 IT deprovisioning
IT disables email, SSO, and VPN access at end-of-day on the last working day. Access to
shared drives and internal tools is revoked at the same time. Personal files on company
devices are not backed up and should be transferred out before the last working day.

§7.2 Email handling after exit
The mailbox is disabled on the last working day and is not forwarded to a personal
address. Where business continuity requires it, the manager may request that incoming
mail be redirected to a named colleague for a limited period. Auto-replies naming a
successor may be set up on request before the last working day.

§7.3 Personal files on company devices
Employees are responsible for removing personal files from company devices before the
last working day. Company devices are wiped and reissued after return, and personal data
on them cannot be recovered afterwards. Employees must not copy company data to personal
storage while removing personal files.

§7.4 Data retention after exit
Work files, mailboxes, and chat history are retained under the company's data retention
schedule and remain company property. Retained data may be accessed by authorised staff
for legal, audit, or business continuity reasons. Former employees have no right of
access to this data after exit.

§7.5 Software licences and accounts
Paid software licences, SaaS seats, and external subscriptions assigned to the employee
are reclaimed on the last working day. Any subscription bought with company funds under
a personal account must be disclosed during KT so it can be transferred, or the cost is
recovered in the final settlement.

§7.6 Physical access and premises
Access cards, parking passes, locker keys, and any building credentials are deactivated
and returned on or before the last working day. Former employees visiting a company site
after exit are treated as visitors and must be signed in by a host.

§8.1 Documents you must submit
Before HR clearance can close, the employee must submit the signed resignation
acknowledgement, the confidentiality acknowledgement, bank details for the final
settlement, a current personal email address and postal address, and any pending
reimbursement claims with receipts.

§8.2 Document validation
Submitted documents are checked for legibility, completeness, and a match against the
employee record. A document that fails validation is returned with the reason and must
be resubmitted. Clearance does not progress while a required document is missing or
rejected.

§8.3 Keeping contact details current
Former employees should keep their personal email and postal address up to date with HR
until the final settlement and tax documents have been issued. Documents are sent to the
last address on record, and the company is not responsible for items sent to an address
the employee did not update.

§9.1 Final settlement
Final settlement, including any pending salary, unused leave encashment, and
reimbursements, is processed within 45 days of the last working day, once all
clearances are complete. Settlement is paid to the bank account on file with HR.

§9.2 What the final settlement includes
The settlement includes salary for days worked in the final month, encashment of unused
earned leave, approved reimbursements, and any bonus or incentive that has already been
declared and is payable under its own scheme rules. Amounts not yet declared are not
included.

§9.3 Deductions and recoveries
Deductions may include notice period shortfall, unreturned or damaged assets at
replacement cost, outstanding salary or travel advances, corporate card balances, excess
leave taken, and any contractual recovery such as a training bond or joining bonus
clawback. All deductions are itemised in the settlement statement.

§9.4 Provident fund and tax documents
Provident fund withdrawal or transfer requests are processed separately from the final
settlement and can take up to 60 days. Form 16 and other tax documents are issued
within the same financial year's filing window, typically by June.

§9.5 Gratuity
Employees who have completed the qualifying period of continuous service under
applicable law are eligible for gratuity, which is calculated on last drawn salary and
completed years of service. Gratuity is paid separately from the final settlement and
may follow a different timeline.

§9.6 Payslips and salary records
Payslips for the notice period and the final settlement statement are issued to the
employee's personal email address on record. Employees should download and keep their
payslips before the last working day, as portal access ends when accounts are
deprovisioned.

§9.7 Reimbursement claim deadline
All pending reimbursement claims must be submitted with valid receipts before the last
working day. Claims received after that date are considered only at the discretion of
Finance and cannot delay the final settlement.

§9.8 Settlement disputes
An employee who disagrees with the final settlement should raise it in writing with HR
within 30 days of receiving the settlement statement. HR reviews the calculation with
Finance and responds with a written explanation or a corrected payment.

§10.1 Health insurance and medical cover
Company-provided health insurance ends on the last working day unless the policy
document states otherwise. Employees who want continuing cover should arrange a personal
policy or a portability request before that date. Claims for treatment before the last
working day remain valid under the policy terms.

§10.2 Other benefits and memberships
Meal cards, wellness benefits, transport allowances, professional memberships, and
learning subscriptions funded by the company end on the last working day. Unused
balances that are not refundable under the provider's terms cannot be encashed.

§11.1 Exit interview
HR schedules an exit interview during the final week of employment. Attendance is
mandatory for all confirmed employees. The interview is confidential and its content
is used only for internal retention analysis, never shared with the employee's manager.

§11.2 What happens to exit interview responses
Responses are aggregated with others to identify trends in retention and employee
experience. Individual responses are visible only to authorised HR staff. HR's internal
assessment notes arising from the interview are not shared with the employee, the
manager, or any other department.

§11.3 Raising a concern during the exit interview
An employee may use the exit interview to raise a concern about conduct, safety, or
policy. Concerns of this kind are handled under the company's grievance and whistleblower
procedures, separately from the exit process, and are investigated even after the last
working day.

§12.1 Relieving and experience letter
The relieving letter is issued once all three clearances (HR, IT, Finance) are marked
complete. The experience letter, confirming role, tenure, and last drawn salary, is
issued within 7 working days of the relieving letter.

§12.2 Background verification and references
The company responds to background verification requests from future employers by
confirming role, tenure, and last drawn salary only. Performance details and internal
assessments are never disclosed. Requests should be directed to HR rather than to former
managers or colleagues.

§12.3 Duplicate or reissued letters
A former employee may request a duplicate relieving or experience letter from HR at any
time. Duplicates restate the original facts and are normally issued within 10 working
days. Letters are not amended or reworded after issue except to correct a factual error.

§13.1 Confidentiality after exit
Employees remain bound by the confidentiality and non-disclosure terms of their
employment contract after their last working day. This survives the termination of
employment and applies regardless of the reason for exit.

§13.2 Intellectual property and work product
All work product, code, designs, documents, and inventions created in the course of
employment remain the property of the company. Employees must not retain copies after
exit and must disclose during KT the location of any work stored outside approved
company systems.

§13.3 Non-solicitation
For the period stated in the employment contract, a former employee must not solicit
company employees to leave, and must not solicit clients they served using company
confidential information. This restriction applies as written in the contract.

§13.4 Return and deletion of company data
On or before the last working day, employees must return or delete all company data in
their possession, including documents, customer lists, source code, and credentials held
on personal devices or personal cloud storage. Retaining company data after exit is a
breach of contract.

§13.5 Representing the company after exit
Former employees must not represent themselves as speaking for or acting on behalf of
the company after the last working day. Public profiles should be updated to show the
correct end date and that the role has ended.

§14.1 Rehire eligibility
Rehire eligibility is assessed internally by HR based on performance history and exit
conduct, and is not disclosed to the departing employee or their manager.

§14.2 Reapplying for a role
A former employee may apply for any open role through the normal recruitment process.
Applications are considered on merit alongside all other candidates and are subject to
HR's internal rehire assessment, the outcome of which is not shared with the applicant.

§15.1 Who to contact
Questions about notice, documents, the exit interview, and letters go to HR. Questions
about knowledge transfer and reassignment of work go to the reporting manager. Questions
about assets and access go to IT. Questions about settlement, deductions, and
reimbursements go to Finance.

§15.2 Grievance and escalation
An employee who believes a stage of their exit is being handled unfairly or is stalled
should raise it with HR in writing. If the outcome is unsatisfactory, it may be escalated
to the HR lead. Raising a grievance does not pause the notice period or the last working
day.

§15.3 Using the exit assistant
The exit assistant answers questions using only this policy and cites the sections it
used. If the answer is not in this policy the assistant will say so rather than guess,
and the question can be forwarded to HR for a written reply. The assistant cannot see or
disclose internal HR assessments.
